## The challenge

[Platform name] is a critical digital channel for capturing and distributing
compulsory and voluntary market disclosures across listed companies and
exchange-traded funds. It also provides access to structured and unstructured
announcements, supporting material, listing rules, waivers, company details,
user administration and profile management.

Over time, deficiencies developed across features, experience, data
management, security, operational needs and analytics. The technology was no
longer fit for purpose and was reaching the end of its production support
lifecycle.

Customer identities and credentials were stored in multiple places, and
authentication differed across channels. Without consistent single sign-on or
multi-factor authentication, auditing of access decisions was limited.

Initial research and discovery also showed we needed a scalable platform
where new digital disclosures could be added incrementally. It had to support
a broader automation strategy: less reliance on unstructured PDFs, more
accurate and timely disclosures, and straight-through processing.

How could we preserve essential features, capabilities and regulatory
integrity, and remediate ageing technology and high security risks, while
creating a user-centric experience that reflects real workflows?

> **Our ambition:** To evolve the channel from an operational portal into a
> product capable of delivering insights, improving engagement, reducing
> customer effort, and raising the quality of interactions with listed
> entities.

## The key usability problem

The most consequential usability problem was embedded in the platform's
underlying identity model.

The existing access model linked identity to an issuer code rather than
consistently defining the individual. One person could therefore hold
multiple login IDs to manage different organisations or issuer codes.

That system model did not match customer behaviour.

Our research and data analysis identified a broad spectrum of relationships:

- Individuals representing a single entity
- Individuals representing multiple entities
- Professional representatives acting for dozens of entities
- Third-party providers working on behalf of client organisations
- People holding different roles and permissions across organisations and products
- Multiple people sharing legacy account credentials
- Individuals maintaining several entity-specific login identities
- Actions belonging to the individual rather than a company
- Regulatory actions, submissions and data belonging to a represented entity or instrument

## The shift

- **From:** one issuer code, one login
- **To:** one individual identity with authorised relationships across one or
  many organisations, products and instruments

The future model needed to support:

- A person and their primary organisation
- Secondary organisations they were permitted to represent
- Internal users and external third-party providers
- Listed and unlisted companies
- Products and instruments such as ETFs and bonds
- Delegated and potentially time-bound access
- One-to-one and one-to-many relationships
- Task-first and company-first workflows
- Individual preferences and entity-owned settings
- Submissions, approvals, notifications and lifecycle states

This sharpened our challenge into a clearer question:

> How might we maintain feature continuity, security and compliance while
> replacing an issuer-bound access model with one individual identity capable
> of operating safely across one or many authorised entities?

## Translating to contextual layers

The relationship model explored how an individual could authenticate once,
select one or more companies or instruments, choose a task, complete the
information, and submit it within the correct authorised context. It
explicitly recognised that both task-first and company-first mental models
needed to be supported.

I translated that relationship model into four persistent contextual layers:

1. **Individual:** Who is authenticated?
2. **Entity or instrument:** Who are they acting for?
3. **Task or record:** What form, announcement, submission or workflow are they completing?
4. **State:** Is the record draft, submitted, approved, rejected, accepted, published, withdrawn, or in another controlled state?

Every regulatory task requires all four layers of context, and users need to
be able to pass tasks between one another within that structure.

A second question followed: how should the experience distinguish between
what belongs to the individual and what belongs to the entity or instrument
they represent? The answer was a clear ownership boundary.

## Validating the idea

Once we'd established the relationship model, the work shifted from
conceptual correctness to practical usability. We explored multiple
iterations of:

- Entity and instrument switching
- Task-first and company-first navigation
- Top-navigation and side-navigation approaches
- Dashboard and landing-page structures
- Contextual hierarchy and labelling
- Notifications and action entry points
- Submission and approval states
- Individual and company settings
- Permission visibility
- Movement between tasks, entities and applications
- Feedback, confirmation and error states

We wanted to create something new enough to support the future platform, but
familiar enough for users to trust their instincts.

## Final solution

### One digital identity

The experience separates a person's identity from the entities they
represent, removing the need to treat every issuer relationship as a separate
human identity. The target state supports a single digital identity, MFA,
federated access, consistent authentication and entitlement-governed
visibility.

### Multi-entity and multi-product access

After authentication, users operate within an authorised organisational
profile. Their role and entitlements determine which organisations, products,
data and actions are available. This includes:

- Organisation access requests
- Third-party invitations
- Primary and secondary organisational relationships
- Product-level roles
- Company and instrument selection
- Applying submissions to one or multiple companies and products

### Context that travels with the task

The relationship between individual, entity, task and state is maintained as
users navigate, receive notifications, hand work between colleagues and
complete approvals. This reduces the risk of users acting for the wrong
entity or losing context during sensitive compliance work.

### Role- and policy-based access

Roles are modelled as collections of permissions governed by least-privilege
principles. Access is evaluated against the organisation, product, role,
entitlement and contextual policy, rather than relying on separate
credentials.

### Compliance embedded into the workflow

The experience makes compliant behaviour easier through:

- Immediate validation
- Clear submission states
- Approval controls
- Traceable amendments and corrections
- Audit histories
- Entity and instrument selection warnings
- Document checks
- Structured information capture
- Proactive alerts, reminders and guidance
- Transparent errors and recovery paths

## Baseline and measuring success

The existing channel had 11,956 activated users. Average weekday traffic
across 2024/25 was around 20,000 page views per day, and in November 2025
daily views ranged from 15,000 to 60,000.

Post-launch, we'll track the measures tied most directly to the problem:

- Authentication and account-switching effort
- Task completion time
- Disclosure error and rework rate
- Multi-entity access adoption
- Support case volume

## Reflection

We began with detailed current-state mapping and service blueprinting. That
work made it possible to separate regulatory necessity from accidental
complexity introduced by legacy technology.

By reframing the problem around users, we developed a relationship framework
connecting individuals, organisations, instruments, roles, permissions, tasks
and states, rather than letting these be shaped by our legacy structures and
data models. This changed the programme from a screen redesign into the
design of a future operating model for a regulated digital ecosystem.

## Next horizon

The model is built to grow: adding more digital disclosures incrementally,
capturing higher-quality machine-readable information, and increasing
immediate validation and automated guidance. Longer term, it opens the door
to API-driven exchange of compliance information, stronger two-way customer
engagement, and insights drawn from portfolio and industry data.
