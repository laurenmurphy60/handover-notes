## Building a buying journey within a digital insurance platform

The business had an opportunity to dive into direct-to-consumer professional
indemnity insurance in the allied health space, with the commitment to
produce a market-competitive offering. Given this, the challenge lay around
creating an on-par, if not superior, user experience with our technology
stack.

### Users & Audiences

To define our audience, we looked at the segments across the insurance
product that had the most potential by evaluating search volumes and
competitors in this space. Our key users were defined as: Beauticians, Physiotherapists, and other
allied health professionals.

### Scope & Constraints

What's possible is defined by current technology and the timeframe we were
working in. All of the work was subject to sign-off from multiple
stakeholders.

## Process

### UX Research

The research began with a full competitor analysis from three different
lenses — price, product and UX. We had a unique opportunity to work with an
underwriting agency on the product, so this analysis was necessary to
create the best product and vessel to sell that product.

#### Competitor Analysis

**Competitors evaluated:** AON, BizCover, CGU, Allianz

**Aspects considered**

- Steps and screens for the user
- How long it takes to receive an initial quote and a final quote
- Core products
- Additional products offered and modalities
- Where and how business details are presented
- How personal details are used and followed up
- Additional comments and notes

#### Steps & Screens — minimising questions and grouping effectively

**Findings**

1. Competitors average 4–5 sections of information per journey.
2. Approx. 4 "submits" / new screens before a quote is displayed — because
   of a mobile focus, users submit more times than they would with a
   desktop-first approach.
3. Competitors are favouring clicks/submits rather than scroll.

**Recommendation**

Effectively group and present questions, then use a combination of scroll
and submit to create an optimised journey that performs on desktop and
mobile.

#### Core and Additional products + Modalities

**Findings**

1. All our competitors across allied health (and in other areas) use
   search as a selection method.
2. We have a wide variety of occupations/selections that the user needs
   to make and feel confident in, in order to continue and obtain an
   accurate quick quote.
3. From a legal and insurance-binding perspective, it reduces liability
   when the user can be specific about the activities we undertake.

**Recommendation**

Develop a search component, similar to those of our competitors, that can
facilitate multiple user journeys across occupations and modalities.

### Collecting personal information

**Findings**

1. 3/4 major competitors required the user to submit their personal
   details (name, contact number, address and email) within the first
   four screens of the user journey, before a quote could be generated.
2. Aon sent a follow-up email once the application was left incomplete
   (before checkout). This indicated that the form captured personal
   data regardless of whether the form was completed or not.

**Conclusion**

Noting that in this space, address and ABN upfront can be used for
targeting/data collection — something to consider in relation to
marketing tactics and personal information. Asking for personal details
upfront is dependent on having the internal support to follow up the lead
if the user drops off after viewing the quote. Asking the user to provide
their email address upfront ensures that if they drop off when asked for
their personal information, you have a contact to follow up.

**Recommendation**

The UX recommendation was to ask for sensitive information following the
initial quote, before the user is taken to checkout. A/B testing should be
conducted to see how well the placement of asking for personal information
performs.

### Learnings from previous work

**Context**

Our current online fitness professionals audience has a very similar
profile to allied health, hence these observations are a good
approximation of allied health behaviour.

**Findings**

1. In the last 6 months, we can attribute 34% of Fitness revenue to mobile.
2. If we take into account people with multi-visit journeys, mobile's
   contribution to our revenue can be brought up to over 40%.
3. Data and behaviour patterns show that users come, get a quote, then
   return to purchase. This would suggest the effectiveness of the quick
   quote in converting users.

**Conclusion**

Desktop and mobile should be treated with equal importance during design
and development.
