## Overview

Working within restricted environments, there's often a need to develop
methods to overcome data and resource constraints. Personas are an
important part of user journey development and generally a crucial UX
artefact — but not always readily available.

Proto-personas can be created when there isn't enough resourcing to build
and validate personas, and are often used as a starting point to guide and
delve deeper into user research. They can help inform initial surveys and
interviews, wireframes, or even support the development of an MVP.

### What makes up a proto persona

Proto-personas are our instinctive understanding of who is using our
product or service and why. They are created using perception and
anecdotal evidence, based on the assumptions of stakeholders, and
ultimately represent what we think our users are like.

True personas are generally based on qualitative and quantitative research,
whereas proto-personas are formed on opinion. A proto-persona can be put
together through the collaborative efforts of stakeholders, designers,
marketers, or anyone who has touchpoints with the users or potential users.

The group will share what they know, or what they think they know, about
the users' demographics, goals, motives and beliefs to create a profile.

### How they can help

Proto-persona workshops often help align stakeholder views on the
user/customer. They also create empathy for the users' needs among
stakeholders, therefore promoting buy-in for user-centric designs. The
value gained vs. the effort required is high.

## Where to start

**Get everyone in a room together**

Stakeholders should be involved, along with anyone else who has key
customer/user insights and touchpoints.

**Make sure they've done some homework.** Before holding the workshop, send
some questions out to your attendees to get them thinking, and have them
bring any prior work or research.

### Engaging workshop participants

**Pre-workshop questions**

1. What is your product and who is your main audience?
2. How do you think your audience segments? Are those clear segments?
3. Currently, where do you find / how do you interact with your audience?
4. Describe your ideal user — who are they, what kind of job do they have?

**Running the workshop**

Start with the workshop persona template, as it gives everyone a good idea
of where they're going and what they're trying to achieve.

Title or occupation is a good place to start, then move on to gender and
age. If no one is agreeing, or nothing is particularly concise, then you
could split your personas by the kinds of problems they are experiencing or
what motivates them.

If you have a group of varying levels of experience and different
personalities, a good way to encourage engagement would be to pick a
scribe for each personality. While you mediate, the scribe takes down
points and makes decisions on what gets included in each persona.
