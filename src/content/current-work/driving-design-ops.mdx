## Addressing the problem

### Project Overview

With limited data and access to our users, using thematic analysis as an
iterative process ensured we get the most out of what we have. By
organising the abstract into concrete themes, we aimed to make sense of our
observations and generate better solutions for our users. The hierarchies
of those themes then help prioritize opportunities for design.

Our contact form was one of the few touch points we had with users through
our B2C website. By analysing the queries submitted, we hoped to build
empathy with our users and create solutions that result in a better user
experience as well as an uplift in conversions/policies sold.

**Users identified**

- Users making submissions through the 'Contact us' form
- Users making submissions through the 'Request a quote' form

### Goals

**Overall goals for the piece of work**

**Research goals**

1. Improve churn rate and drop off through this specific area of the website
2. Enhance and provide more relevant content for our users
3. Improve goal completion (online sales and offline enquiries)
4. Aid in the nurturing / sales of policies offline (direct contact with an insurance broker)

### Scope & Constraints

- Small sample of data
- Unable to match data with current client base due to privacy around conflicting technologies
- Unable to determine accurate conversion rates from these enquiries

## Process

### Categorisation of data

We examined the forms and their categorisation of data, which we
classified by the business segment and type of enquiry. We also looked at
how data were categorised by our users. For example, we used tags to group
content by subject and topic.

The group concluded that these were different types of categorical
(qualitative) data:

- **Business segment** — this is a classification based on where the
  enquiries came from, such as enquiries from customers or prospects in a
  specific region, product or service area.
- **Type of enquiry** — this is a classification based on the type of
  question asked (for example, about your company or product).

As each business segment has fairly specific needs, we then dove into the
type of enquiries and how they were split.

The enquiries around our fitness insurance product could be categorised
into 6 different types:

1. Multiple Employees
2. Qualifications
3. Additional Services
4. Group Sessions
5. Small Classes
6. Personal Training

## Outcomes & Implementation

We discovered that there was content in our FAQs that was overlooked on
the page. This allowed us to create a list of FAQs which we would then
categorise by topic, making it easier for customers to find the content
they were looking for. It was also discovered that many customers were
confused about the insurance that they were purchasing on the site, so we
used this as a starting point for more extensive research around our
customers' interaction with the buying journey.
